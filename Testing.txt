SCOPE
Scope Message
We will accept reports on any asset that is within Crypto.com's control. For assets outside of our control, like vendors (e.g. https://help.crypto.com), we will accept reports if the vulnerability was caused due to a misconfiguration by us.
Out of Scope: User and Personnel Targeting
We strictly prohibit any attacks targeting our users, employees, or staff. This is out of scope and includes, but is not limited to:
Deceptive Software: Publishing or distributing malicious or baiting software, including IDE extensions (e.g., VSCode), browser extensions, AI skills, or public code packages.
Social Engineering: Attempting to trick or socially engineer staff or users into installing third-party tools or clicking links.
Unauthorized Data Collection: Extracting any data, beacons, or telemetry from our users' or staff members' machines, regardless of whether the data is deemed "harmless" or benign.
Malware: Any attacks involving viruses, trojans, or destructive payloads.

Smart Contracts
For vulnerabilities against in-scope smart contracts, please note that we score internally only based on potential impact to user funds and cryptographic security rather than CVSS 3.1. As always, all reports must come with a valid Proof-of-Concept. This means that reports operating on theoretical attacks such as governance attacks, incorrect data supplied by third-party oracles, sybil attacks, or lacks of liquidity are not considered valid reports.
We only consider the latest mainnet releases of blockchain assets as in-scope. We will not consider reports against assets marked "Mock" or "Test". Please note that any report which included draining another individual's funds or blocking them access to their funds without their consent will automatically make you ineligible for this program.

WORK PLAN AND GUARDRAILS
- First compare the requested targets and actions against the written scope. Verify ownership of any newly discovered assets with recon-scope-triage before testing them.
- If scope, authorization, rate limits, or an important restriction is missing or ambiguous, ask me before active testing. Do not guess.
- Begin with reconnaissance and keep a concise recon-notes.md. Continue to active testing only within the explicit program rules.
- Check whether each required tool is installed. Follow TOOLS.md for fallbacks. Ask me before installing packages or binaries; do not use elevated privileges unless I explicitly approve them.
- No denial-of-service, flooding, brute-force, destructive changes, real-user account access, unnecessary collection of personal data, or actions that cause charges, emails, or other external side effects.
- Use test accounts and controlled data wherever possible. Stop at the minimum non-destructive proof of impact. Do not dump datasets, retrieve cloud credentials, persist, evade detection, or test lateral movement.
- Treat automated scanner output as leads only. Verify each candidate manually with a minimal, replayable request before calling it a finding.
- Keep credentials and raw sensitive evidence out of reports, screenshots, shared logs, and committed files. Redact tokens, cookies, and unnecessary personal data.
- Do not submit anything to HackerOne or contact the program on my behalf. Prepare findings for my review and submission.

DELIVERABLES
Write these files under the output directory above:
- pentest-report.md — concise summary, scope, methods, verified findings, severity rationale, exact replayable PoCs, impact, remediation, and honest coverage limitations.
- recon-notes.md — assets and endpoints found, with ownership/scope status and tools used.
- poc-requests.txt — minimal reproduction requests with all credentials and sensitive values replaced by placeholders such as <REDACTED_TOKEN>.

For every candidate finding, record the exact request and a minimal response excerpt, confirm the impact (not just an unusual status code), and run the request again before reporting it. Separate confirmed findings from unconfirmed leads. State what was not tested and why. Use the severity/CVSS version required by the program policy.

Start by confirming that the scope and constraints above are sufficient. If they are, summarize the allowed targets and proposed first phase, then begin reconnaissance.
